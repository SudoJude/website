## Project Overview: Gaft.ie

The housing crisis has made finding student accommodation a difficult, stressful process characterised by high rents and long commutes. This project involved designing a website, Gaft.ie, to serve as a one-stop shop for finding different types of student accommodation and housemates. The core goal was to transition the student housing experience from a fragmented search engine into a single digital platform that also acts as a management companion for the duration of a lease.

## Exploration/Discovery

I benchmarked two existing platforms, Daft.ie and the UL Student Accommodation portal. Through user testing, I identified several shared friction points. Both sites utilised sign-in walls at critical moments, such as when a user wanted to contact a landlord or access help. Pricing consistency was poor, with listings mixing per-week and per-month rates. Users found the UL portal visually cluttered with an overwhelming number of filters, while Daft.ie lacked student-specific filters and obscured results with large advertisement banners

## Design Process

Using the research outcomes, I developed affinity diagrams and need statements to establish a "search-first" architecture. I removed mandatory account creation for browsing and mapped out a user flow using low-fidelity sketched wireframes. The design prioritised information hierarchy, moving essential data like total cost and distance to campus to the forefront of the UI. I structured the platform around three pillars: transparent discovery, financial clarity, and formalised communication.

## Final Design

The final Figma prototype features a clean, scannable layout. The discovery phase includes standardised per-month pricing, clear academic-year availability, and an integrated map view. Beyond discovery, the "MyStay" dashboard acts as a central hub for tenants. It includes a payment portal for rent, a utility splitter that divides and tracks bills among housemates, and a problem reporting system that allows users to log maintenance issues directly with landlords to maintain a digital paper trail.

## Impact & Reflection

Building this project was a steep learning curve in Figma (which I had never used before), struggling with layouts and element sizing initially forced me to pay much closer attention to component consistency across the UI. User testing the prototype was eye-opening, as it highlighted that users prioritise high volumes of practical information, like available properties, over extra features. The process also demonstrated the importance of visual communication, showing me I need to balance technical design execution with stronger presentation materials for my final work
